# Eatlyte Nutrition Product Vision

Eatlyte is a commercial nutrition tracker and family wellness app. It must combine strong software engineering with safe nutrition guidance, dietitian-style recommendations, family wellness tracking, and a polished mobile-first experience.

## Non-medical wellness positioning
Eatlyte provides general wellness and nutrition education. It must not diagnose, treat, or replace licensed medical care. For children, pregnancy, elderly users, diabetes, kidney disease, heart disease, eating disorders, allergies, medication-related concerns, or other chronic conditions, the product should recommend consulting a licensed doctor or registered dietitian.

## Core nutrition outcomes
Eatlyte should help users:
- Track meals, calories, macros, fiber, sugar, sodium, hydration, weight, and key micronutrients.
- Understand meal balance and food quality.
- Build consistent healthy habits without shame or extreme recommendations.
- Personalize nutrition goals by age, gender, height, weight, activity, dietary preference, cuisine preference, allergies, family role, and daily routine.
- Support Indian and global food patterns, homemade meals, packaged foods, restaurant foods, and budget-friendly choices.

## Individual Dietitian Mode
Individual mode should prioritize:
- Daily dashboard with calories remaining, protein progress, carbs, fats, water, fiber, today's meals, score explanation, smart insight, weekly trend, and goal progress.
- Meal-by-meal nutrition breakdown.
- Explainable nutrition score from real logs.
- Healthy swaps and habit reminders.
- Weekly trends that encourage improvement rather than judgment.

## Family Dietitian Mode
Family mode should support:
- Separate profiles for each family member.
- Individual calorie and macro targets.
- Child-friendly and elderly-friendly tracking.
- Shared grocery suggestions and family meal planning.
- Alerts for low protein, low fiber, high sugar, high sodium, missed meals, and hydration gaps.
- A simple daily family wellness summary.

## Nutrition score requirements
The nutrition score must never be fixed or hardcoded. It should be calculated from real user data, including calories versus goal, protein versus target, carb/fat balance, fiber, water, sugar, sodium, meal consistency, fruit/vegetable intake, ultra-processed food frequency, and activity data where available.

Every score must be explainable with clear reasons and suggested next actions.

## Food database requirements
Food search should be robust and practical, supporting common foods, Indian foods, homemade meals, packaged foods, restaurant-style foods, barcode-based foods, custom foods, recipes, serving adjustment, and unit conversion such as grams, cups, pieces, bowl, plate, and spoon.

Prefer reliable food data sources such as USDA FoodData Central, Open Food Facts, licensed FatSecret data where configured, and a verified internal food database.

## Feature design checklist
For every nutrition-related feature, document or consider:
1. User benefit.
2. Nutrition value.
3. Technical implementation.
4. Database impact.
5. UI/UX impact.
6. Safety considerations.
7. Testing checklist.

## Experience principles
Eatlyte should feel trustworthy, clean, professional, friendly, family-oriented, health-focused, easy to understand, and motivating without being judgmental.
